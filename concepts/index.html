Firefighter robot: body concepts A to E

Design review draft · 7 October 2026 · status: planned, nothing built yet
See also: progress report, 8 October 2026

We want a robot that walks to a fire and puts it out with a water nozzle or, as a fallback, a water gun. The body reuses the legs and torso frame of the open-source Berkeley Humanoid Lite. This document shows five ways to add the water system, the cameras and the aiming. Every concept is drawn fully assembled so we can compare them before we buy parts.

The concepts differ in three choices: where the water comes from, how the robot aims, and what sits on top of the hips.

1 Overview

2 Shared base

All five concepts stand on the Berkeley Humanoid Lite legs: 6 motors per leg and about 0.8 m tall. In the robot's URDF file, the legs and the torso frame weigh 11.3 kg. Concept C also keeps the two arms.

Concepts B to E carry an RGB camera and a small thermal camera side by side. Concept A has a single RGB camera fixed on the gun.

The added masses below are rough estimates. Nobody has weighed or tested any of these parts yet.

3 Concepts

3.1 Concept A: Water gun turret

An electric toy water gun on a two-servo pan-tilt mount on top of the torso. The gun keeps its own tank and pump. We remove the handle and connect the trigger to a relay or a MOSFET. A USB camera sits on the gun, so the camera image shows where the water goes.

WaterGun's own tank, about 0.5 L
AimPan-tilt, 2 servos
Added mass~1.4 kg
Build effortLow

Pros

  • Fastest and cheapest build. No pump or hoses to design.
  • Good first demo while the walking works.

Cons

  • Short range and low flow.
  • The gun sits high, so the center of mass goes up.

3.2 Concept B: Backpack pump

A clear tank and a 12 V diaphragm pump on a back frame. The head carries the cameras and the nozzle. The nozzle is fixed under the cameras, so the robot shoots where it looks. After one calibration, the center of the camera image is the aim point. The pump sits above the tank and primes itself.

Water1.4 L tank on the back
AimHead pan-tilt, 2 servos
Added mass~2.7 kg
Build effortMedium

Pros

  • More water and more pressure than A.
  • One aim system for vision and water.

Cons

  • The water on the back moves the center of mass backward. We must train the walking policy with this load.
  • Water moves in the tank while the robot walks.

3.3 Concept C: Arm lance

The full Berkeley Humanoid Lite with arms. The right arm holds a nozzle lance, and the head wears a helmet with cameras. The arm already exists in the Berkeley design, with 5 motors. The hose runs from the backpack pump over the right shoulder and along the arm. The left arm can help with balance.

Water1.0 L backpack tank and pump
AimRight arm, 5 motors
Added mass~2.3 kg + 5.0 kg of arms
Build effortHigh

Pros

  • Most human-like. Best for a presentation.
  • The arm can aim in many directions and around obstacles.

Cons

  • Heaviest concept, about 18.6 kg.
  • The arm must hold the lance against the push of the water jet.
  • Most control work: arm and balance together.

3.4 Concept D: Sealed shell

A reflective shell covers the torso. The nozzle is fixed in the chest, and the robot aims with its whole body. The tank, pump, battery and computer are inside the shell. The robot turns with its hip motors to aim left and right, and bends its legs to aim up and down. A gauge on the side shows the water level.

WaterAbout 1 L inside the shell
AimWhole body, no extra motors
Added mass~2.3 kg
Build effortMedium to high

Pros

  • Protects the electronics from water and heat.
  • No extra motors that can break. Carry handle on top.

Cons

  • Slow and coarse aim. The robot must stop and turn to aim.
  • The shell needs air vents for the electronics.

3.5 Concept E: Tethered hose

No water on board. A hose from an external pump or a tap connects to the back of the pelvis. A solenoid valve on the pan-tilt mount opens the nozzle. The camera sits on a short mast. A power cable can run next to the hose, so the robot can also run without a battery.

WaterNo limit, from outside
AimPan-tilt, 2 servos
Added mass~1.0 kg + hose pull
Build effortLow to medium

Pros

  • Lightest robot. No limit on water or run time.
  • An outside pump can give real hose pressure.

Cons

  • The hose pulls the robot back and can catch the legs.
  • The hose length limits the range, and the hose twists when the robot turns.

5 Suggested next step

Build A first. It needs the least new work and gives us a working demo early. We should design its top plate so that the head of B or the valve of E can replace the gun later.

Before we add any load, we should test the extra mass in simulation. The walking policy was trained without it.

6 Notes on the renders

The renders come from Blender 5.2 (Cycles). The legs, torso and arms are the real Berkeley Humanoid Lite meshes, placed from the URDF in the default standing pose (hip −0.2 rad, knee 0.4 rad, ankle −0.3 rad). The gun, tanks, pumps, heads and shell are simple placeholder shapes with approximate sizes.
